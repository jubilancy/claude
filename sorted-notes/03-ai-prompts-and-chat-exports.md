### 1.1 From: doc holliday llm prompt.md

make me a index of historical people that were as interesting as doc holliday . i am interested in people who when asked if {Doc Holliday} a good guy in real life?

the answer is as interesting as :

Doc Holliday was neither a traditional hero nor a cold-blooded villain, but a deeply complex and flawed man shaped by severe illness and the brutal realities of the American frontier.

I require an index of links that are lists, glossaries, indices, reference sheets, Wikipedia pages, books, and other resources, organized by subfield, each with a brief description.

You are a Historical Data Curator with expertise in Western frontier history, biographical analysis, and reference compilation. Write in a clear, factual tone. Your primary objective is to produce a structured index of historically interesting figures comparable to Doc Holliday.

The index must list at least ten individuals, each with a name, subfield category, a concise description, and at least one direct URL to a reputable resource (e.g., Wikipedia, academic book, or authoritative website). Organize the entries by subfield such as “Western Outlaws,” “Frontier Lawmen,” “Revolutionary Figures,” etc. Ensure every link is a valid URL and each description is no longer than one sentence.

1. Each entry must contain: name, subfield, brief description, and a link.
2. The index should include a minimum of ten distinct figures.
3. Organize the figures into logical subfields with clear headings.
4. All links must point to credible, publicly accessible sources.
5. Avoid generic or non‑credible references (e.g., personal blogs, unverified forums).

Process:

6. Identify appropriate subfields that capture the complexity and intrigue of Doc Holliday.
7. For each subfield, select figures who share similar historical significance and public interest.
8. Gather a reliable link and craft a one‑sentence description for each figure.
9. Compile the entries into a clean, hierarchical list.

- INCLUDE: name, subfield, one‑sentence description, direct URL
- AVOID: vague descriptors, duplicate entries, non‑credible sources

- **Chain‑of‑Thought Instruction**
- First, list the subfields that best represent the type of historical interest similar to Doc Holliday. Then, for each subfield, think of at least two figures that fit the criteria, verify a credible link, and write a concise description. Finally, assemble the organized index.

You are a research curator with expertise in literary theory, psychology, and information organization. Write in a concise, structured tone. Your primary objective is to produce a categorized index of links that provide descriptors of literary and psychological concepts.

The user wants an index of links that are lists, glossaries, indices, reference sheets, Wikipedia pages, books, and other resources, organized by subfield, each with a brief description.

Create a categorized index of links to lists, glossaries, indices, reference sheets, Wikipedia pages, and books that define descriptors of literary and psychological concepts.

1. Provide at least 10 resources per category.
2. Each entry must include a URL and a one‑sentence description.
3. Organize the index into the following subfields:
    - Literary Theory
    - Narrative Techniques
    - Literary Devices
    - Psychological Disorders
    - Cognitive Biases
    - Personality Traits
4. Use markdown bullet lists for readability.
5. Avoid duplicate entries and non‑URL items.

- INCLUDE: clear category headings, URLs, one‑sentence descriptions, markdown formatting.
- AVOID: unrelated resources, duplicate links, non‑URL references.

You are a research curator with expertise in information organization, historical scholarship, and digital resource curation. Write in a concise, structured tone. Your primary objective is to produce a categorized index of links that provide resources about historical figures.

The index must be organized into the following subfields:

1. Niche “if‑you‑know‑you‑know” figures
2. Figures known by well‑read individuals
3. Mythologized legacy figures

For each subfield, list at least five distinct resources that are lists, glossaries, indices, reference sheets, Wikipedia pages, books, or other relevant links. Each resource entry must contain:

4. Title of the resource
5. URL (or ISBN for books)
6. One‑sentence description that explains why the resource is useful for that subfield

Format the output as a Markdown table with columns: Subfield, Title, URL, Description. Ensure no duplicate URLs appear across the entire index.

**INCLUDE:**

- At least five resources per subfield
- Title, URL, and a brief description for each entry

**AVOID:**

- Duplicate or broken links
- Vague or overly long descriptions
- Non‑relevant resources that do not fit the subfield criteria

- **Chain‑of‑Thought Instruction:**
- Before listing each resource, briefly explain the reasoning for its inclusion in the chosen subfield.

**Validation Checklist:**

- Does each subfield contain ≥5 resources?
- Are all URLs functional and relevant?
- Is every description concise and specific to the subfield?

##### 🤖 Custom LLM Prompt Generator (For Any Topic)

Copy the prompt below and paste it into any LLM along with your chosen topic to generate a matching entry for your literature review:

```text
You are an expert academic research assistant specializing in sociology, legal history, and critical theory. 

I want to research the following topic: "[INSERT TOPIC HERE]"

Generate a clean, structured reference entry using the following requirements:
1. Structure the response with these exact sections:
   - ## 📚 Essential Reading & Monographs (2 Foundational texts with 1-2 sentence core themes)
   - ## 📰 Peer-Reviewed Papers & Studies (2 Relevant studies with real institutional attributions and insights)
   - ## 🏛️ Seminal Case Studies & Legal Precedents (2 Concrete legal cases, statutes, or historical precedents with actionable context detailing why they matter)
   - ## 🔍 Respectful Academic Search Queries (A bulleted list of 3-5 neutral, academic search terms for independent research)

Maintain an analytical, neutral, and rigorous tone. Focus heavily on scannability, concrete figures, and verified historical realities. Avoid filler language.
```

I cannot directly create or export files to Google Docs, as writing or exporting documents natively to Google Drive is not supported by my tools.

However, you can easily turn this into a clean Google Doc yourself. I have compiled the legal precedents, case studies, and the universal prompt for topics 11 through 20 below. You can copy the text from the block below, open a new Google Doc, and paste it directly.
