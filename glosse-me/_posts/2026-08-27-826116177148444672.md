> Have you ever wondered how long those nice people were actually stranded on the Orient Express? Or speculated about the carriage distance from London to Longbourn? Well, dear reader, have I got a tip for you.
>
> [Reading Maps](https://readingmaps.com/) is home to an interactive database where curious readers can explore—if not recreate—famous journeys from literature.
>
> These trips are classified by mode (overland, sea) and type (survival, exile, coming of age). There are road trips represented, like that in Dave Eggers’ [*Heartbreaking Work of Staggering Genius](https://readingmaps.com/maps/a-heartbreaking-work-of-staggering-genius)*. But also multi-country emigration tales, as in Yaa Gyasi’s [*Homegoing](https://readingmaps.com/maps/homegoing)*.
>
> This project is the brainchild of Aires Loutsaris—UK-based SEO consultant by day, whimsical cartographer by night. Loutsaris has spent the past few years building his free interactive atlas, which includes both intercontinental epics (see: [*Moby Dick](https://readingmaps.com/maps/moby-dick)*) and local meanders like *[Mrs. Dalloway](https://readingmaps.com/maps/mrs-dalloway). *
